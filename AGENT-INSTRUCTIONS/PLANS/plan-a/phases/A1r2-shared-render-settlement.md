**Settlement recorded in this phase:**
1. A1 on this branch is unchanged as detailed: `render(width, height)` with the `WIDTH` and `HEIGHT`
   constants, one `Rng::new(SEED)` in `render`, the 50-bounce material `ray_colour` with
   `sky_colour`, gamma 2 in `to_byte`, and the scene's diffuse centre, diffuse ground and metal
   sphere. The merged tree takes plan-c's `render(width, height, samples)` and its settings and
   command-line path. That is the release merge's work, not a change to A1 on this branch.
2. The merged `render` starts from plan-c's C1 and takes A1's material loop inside it:
   `fn render(width: usize, height: usize, samples: usize) -> Vec<Colour>` builds the scene, the
   camera and one `Rng::new(SEED)`, then for each row from the top and each column computes `u` and
   `v`, traces `samples` rays with `ray_colour`, and pushes their linear average. Exactly one
   generator is created, at the top of `render`, and passed `&mut` through every bounce. There is no
   second generator, global or per-pixel or per-sample reseeding.
3. Nesting is row, then column, then sample, then bounce: one trace per sample, with the same
   generator threaded through every bounce of that trace. Consumption order is row-major, so a fixed
   sample count reproduces byte-identically across runs.
4. `ray_colour` keeps its name and its 50-bounce material loop, returns linear radiance, returns
   black at the bounce limit and the sky gradient on a miss.
5. Averaging happens in linear space in `render`. Gamma 2 comes from A1 on this branch:
   `to_byte` applies `sqrt` once to the averaged channel. The merge keeps one `to_byte` body with
   that `sqrt`, and both the PNG and PPM writers use it. Gamma is never applied per sample.
6. `render` returns the pixel vector unchanged to `run(&Settings)`, which encodes and writes it as
   C1 leaves it. Neither writer changes in this settlement.
7. The progress line is the output plan's C3 two `eprint`/`eprintln` calls inside the row loop
   after the column loop, inside `render`. It counts rows only and is independent of samples and
   bounces. A1 and A3 do not move, rename or re-implement it; the merge keeps plan-c's calls.
8. `samples` is always at least 1 by the time `render` runs: C1's option parser rejects a value below
   1 with the usage line and exit status 2, and `tests/cli.rs` covers `--samples 0`. Nothing in
   `render`, A1 or A3 adds a second guard for it. Once materials consume the generator the image
   depends on the sample count, and each fixed sample count reproduces byte-identically.
9. A3 on this branch creates the sample loop itself, because A1 leaves `render(width, height)` with
   no sample loop at all. A3's branch shape is the frozen `render(width, height, samples)`
   signature, with 16 passed from `main` as this branch's default, one `Rng::new(SEED)` before the
   loops, and the row, column, sample, bounce nesting above. Per sample, before the bounce trace, it
   draws two `next_f64` offsets for `u` and `v` inside the pixel. It averages in linear space and
   leaves gamma in `to_byte`. It adds no sample-count guard. After the merge, A3 keeps the frozen
   signature and the command-line-owned defaults (400 by 200, 16 samples) and its only remaining
   change is the jitter.
10. At the release merge plan-c's identical sample loop wins textually, so the merge deduplicates
    the loop rather than making A3 rely on plan-c's. A3's contribution to the merged tree is the
    jitter draws. The draw order is fixed: for each sample, two `next_f64` draws offset `u` and `v`
    inside the pixel, then the bounce scattering draws for that trace, row-major across the image.
11. The release merge's obligations on plan-a's side:

    - make A1's `to_byte` body the one both writers use;
